## Design principles for intelligent systems

### Properties of embodies agents
- Subject to the laws of physics - interacts with the real world
- generation of sensory stimulation through interaction with real world
- affect the environment through behaviour
- perform morphological computation

- What is an Autonomus agent?
 > Anything that can be viewed as precieving its environemantr through sensors and action upon that environemt ...
 
 
### 1. Three constituents principle
- We must consider three components together
	- environment
	- behaviour or task
	- design of the agent
- **Scaffolding:** all assumptions about our environement can reduce the complexity of the task
### 2. Complete agent principle
 - one must consider the behavioir of the whole agent in its environement, rather than each of its part in isolation
 - see the whole system not single parts, bc. that could lead to falsy assumtions
 
 **Danger:** 
	 - F-O-R problem. what appears to us as two modules might just be one (or more, 20, 30,...)
	 - Don't take into account the interactions of the parts 
	 - Failing to exploit the interactions of the interactions
	 - Do not take advantage of scaffolding

> Always think about the whole agent behaving in the real world
> 
> Every action has a potentially effect on the entire system -> consider that
### 🫰3. Cheap design
- one might build simpler robots that can also do the same task as a complex one
- Do it simple not expensive
- Sometimes it is not necessary to use fancy techniques to reach a behaviour
- e.g. "Brainless robots" 
### 4. Redundancy
- As a complete agent: redundancy against changes in the body and environment (Wiederstandsfähig)
- react to those changes
### 5. Sensor-motor coordination
- Action and perception should not be seperated
- Sensorimotor learning: correlations between sonsory and motor streams are how we learn about our world -> maybe forming internal maps
### ♻️6. Ecological balance
- there is a match between the complexities of the agents sensory, motor, neural system
- balance in these three fields _( sensory, motor, neural system)_
- balance/task distributions
- givent task should match the the abilities of robot abilities

> e.g. ecological unbalanced: A snail with eyes as big as a human eye

### 8. Value
- curiosity and intrinsic motivation
- For a given agent and environment, changing the reward system might completely change the behaviour
